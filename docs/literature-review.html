Scaling Beyond Context: A Survey of Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Document Understanding

ACL 2026 Main Conference (arXiv 2510.15253, v3 Apr 2026)

THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT RECORD FOR THE GAP ARGUMENT, AND IT SUPPORTS THE PROPOSAL RATHER THAN REFUTING IT. Across 48 tabulated multimodal RAG systems, the survey identifies NO system that performs query-conditioned routing across two or more dimensions simultaneously. It explicitly characterises the systems that come closest as doing something else: VisDoMRAG, HM-RAG and ViDoRAG perform per-modality retrieval but combine by UNION OR FUSION rather than routing; MG-RAG and MMRAG-DocQA build hierarchical multi-granularity indexes but do NOT select granularity per query; ViDoRAG, HM-RAG and HEAR use agents for orchestration and iterative refinement rather than an upfront routing decision.

HOW THIS SQUARES WITH UNIVERSALRAG. The survey covers 2020-2025 and is document-focused. UniversalRAG is ACL 2026, concurrent with the survey, and spans text, image and video corpora rather than documents specifically. So the two are consistent: within multimodal DOCUMENT RAG through 2025, an independent survey finds the multi-dimension router absent, and the one clear counterexample sits outside that scope and appeared alongside the survey itself. The gap statement should be scoped to multimodal document RAG and dated, not stated unconditionally.

IT ALSO CONFIRMS THE PATTERN THIS REVIEW FOUND INDEPENDENTLY. Three of this registry's multimodal systems -- VisDoM, ViDoRAG, MMRAG-DocQA -- were each read in full and each turned out to pay for everything and merge. The survey generalises that to the field: union and fusion, not selection, is how multimodal document RAG currently handles several modalities and granularities. That is exactly the cost structure this project's budget framing attacks, and it means the static fusion baseline this project fails to beat is also the field's default.

Modality Relevance is not Modality Utility: Post-hoc Selective Modality Escalation for Cost-Aware Multimodal RAG

arXiv 2607.05438 (cs.IR)

ORACLE STRUCTURE. On MultiModalQA dev, 61% of questions are modality saturated -- their gold support includes an image, yet text and tables alone answer them correctly. Two different oracles follow, and the gap between them is the paper's main point: an oracle that escalates on GOLD IMAGE SUPPORT reaches 0.430 at a 39% escalation rate, while a UTILITY oracle that escalates only where vision actually fixes a wrong answer reaches 0.469 at 12%. The utility oracle beats always-on VLM (0.446), so extra visual evidence can distract. Text+tables alone is 0.351.

CORRECTION TO THIS REGISTRY'S EARLIER CLAIM. The abstract does not mention a random control, and this record previously said none was reported. THE FULL TEXT SHOWS OTHERWISE, and the earlier claim is withdrawn. Table 2 compares three routers -- random escalation, calibrated pre-retrieval, and post-hoc -- under identical 50/50 stratified splits, identical calibration machinery and identical thresholding, with ONLY the feature set differing, each thresholded to hit a target escalation budget on training data, evaluated once on held-out test, averaged over 10 seeds. That is a matched-budget random control, and it is stricter than what this project runs, because the budget is hit by calibration on training data rather than by taking the top-B fraction at evaluation time.

WHAT STILL SEPARATES THIS PROJECT. No permutation control over the action marginals and no tie-aware oracle appear anywhere; significance is handled only as per-seed standard deviation (<=0.006 on the main comparison). Decisions are made POST-HOC on the tuple (query, draft answer, evidence), which costs a generation pass this project's pass-counting budget would charge and which this project's pre-generation cascade does not need. The measured quantity is answer accuracy on MultiModalQA, not evidence recall over document quotes on MMDocRAG.

NUMERICAL PARALLEL WORTH REPORTING. Their utility oracle needs 12% escalation to beat always-on; this project's E36 router reaches always-ColQwen quality at B = 0.05 to 0.15. The two are measuring different things on different corpora and must not be pooled, but the agreement in ORDER OF MAGNITUDE is the strongest external support the project's cost claim currently has.

UniversalRAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation over Corpora of Diverse Modalities and Granularities

ACL 2026

THE ROUTER IS FLAT, NOT HIERARCHICAL. It is a single six-way classifier over {None, Paragraph, Document, Image, Clip, Video} -- modality and granularity are decided in ONE joint prediction, not as a sequence of decisions. The proposal's word 'hierarchical' therefore still describes something this paper does not do.

LABELS COME FROM DATASET IDENTITY, NOT MEASURED OUTCOMES. Training labels are constructed from benchmark inductive biases -- queries from a dataset meant to be answered parametrically are labelled None, single-hop RAG benchmarks are labelled Paragraph, and so on. That is a proxy label, and it is precisely the mistake this project corrected in E17: the original retriever router was trained on gold modality labels with a hardcoded mapping, and only re-training on the per-question recall difference showed what was really learnable. A router trained on which benchmark a query came from cannot be evidence about which corpus that query actually needs.

NO COST IS REPORTED ANYWHERE. No latency, no compute, no retrieval-pass count. So despite being a modality router, it makes no efficiency claim at all -- the budget question this project asks is untouched by it.

NO ALL-MODALITY FUSION BASELINE. Baselines are naive, single-modality variants, a Unified encoder over combined corpora, a random router and an oracle. Nothing retrieves from every modality corpus and fuses the results, which is the static strong baseline this project has to beat (RRF over both branches). The Unified baseline is a shared embedding space, which the paper itself argues suffers a modality gap -- a weaker comparator than rank fusion over separate indices.

HEADLINE. 39.60 average with a DistilBERT router on InternVL2.5-8B, against 31.15 for Unified and 40.34 for the oracle -- an oracle gap of roughly 0.7 to 1.0 average score. Router errors concentrate on the clip-versus-video granularity boundary while the modality decision stays correct, which suggests granularity is the harder axis even here.

ColPali: Efficient Document Retrieval with Vision Language Models

ICLR 2025

THE MOST IMPORTANT SINGLE FINDING FOR THIS PROJECT'S BOUNDARY STATEMENT. ColPali's baselines include a CAPTIONING pipeline in which visual elements are described by Claude-3 Sonnet and then retrieved as text. ColPali reaches 81.3 NDCG@5 averaged over ViDoRe against roughly 67 for the captioning approach -- about fourteen points in favour of reading pixels over reading VLM-written descriptions, at PAGE granularity on whole pages. This project measures no such advantage, and the difference is a setting difference, not a contradiction: E24 evaluates cropped quote regions inside a question-conditioned candidate pool with a median of about twenty image candidates per document, where recall at 20 is already near saturation. The project must therefore state its ColQwen result as bounded to that regime and must NOT generalise it to descriptions being as good as pixels.

IT SUPPLIES THE COST NUMBERS THE TWO-CURRENCY ARGUMENT NEEDS. Storage is 256 KB per page in float16, which the paper itself calls an order of magnitude more than BGE-M3 at 8.6 KB; VisRAG's single-vector retriever is 4.5 KB by comparison. Query encoding is about 30 ms against 22 ms for BGE-M3. So the late-interaction cost is dominated by index size and by scoring, not by query encoding -- which is consistent with this project pricing a ColQwen pass as a distinct currency rather than as a multiple of a BM25 pass.

THE ARCHITECTURE IS THE ONE THIS PROJECT RUNS. PaliGemma-3B backbone, 1,024 patch vectors per page projected to 128 dimensions, scored by summing each query vector's maximum dot product against the page's vectors. Whole pages are the atomic retrieved unit throughout; no cropped figure regions are evaluated anywhere in the paper.

Mix-of-Granularity: Optimize the Chunking Granularity for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

COLING 2025, pp. 5756-5774

THE SUPERVISION IS A PROXY, AND A CAREFUL ONE. For each query the best snippet at each granularity is retrieved with BM25, scored against ground truth by RoBERTa similarity, TF-IDF or hit rate, and turned into SOFT labels -- 0.8 for the best granularity, 0.2 for the second, 0 otherwise -- to keep training differentiable. An MLP over RoBERTa query embeddings predicts a weight vector; selection is soft in training and hard at inference. Five granularities, each level pairing two adjacent chunks of the level below.

THE MISSING BASELINE IS THE SAME ONE THIS PROJECT INSISTS ON. It compares against chain-of-thought without retrieval and against MedRAG, a system with ONE granularity. There is no sweep over fixed granularities, so the reported gain cannot be separated into what comes from having several granularities available and what comes from choosing among them per query. E25/E26 in this project exist precisely because that separation changes the sign of the conclusion.

NO ORACLE ANYWHERE. No upper bound on per-query granularity selection, no measurement of how often the router picks a suboptimal level, no ceiling. The ablation only removes the router and shows performance falling back to MedRAG.

IT DOES PRICE THE ROUTER, WHICH IS RARE AND WORTH CITING. Storage is 2.7x the corpus to hold five granularities of embeddings. Average inference time goes from 8.12 s without the router to 13.16 s with it on a single granularity -- a 60% overhead for the routing decision itself -- and 17.93 s across five granularities. Gains are +1.47 points for GPT-3.5 and +7.48 for Llama3 on MedCorp, larger for weaker backbones.

Benchmarking Retrieval-Augmented Multimodal Generation for Document Question Answering

NeurIPS 2025 (poster)

THE BENCHMARK THIS PROJECT IS BUILT ON, READ AS A SYSTEM RATHER THAN AS A DATASET. 4,055 expert-annotated QA pairs over 222 documents averaging 67 pages, median 28, maximum 844, with multi-page cross-modal evidence chains. 60 VLM/LLM models and 14 retrieval systems are evaluated.

IT IS A STATIC SYSTEM, AND THAT IS WHAT MAKES IT THE RIGHT BASELINE. A fixed modality quota is applied to every query regardless of what that query needs: top-10 is 7 text plus 3 image, top-15 is 10 plus 5, top-20 is 12 plus 8. Nothing is conditioned on the question. The project's whole budget argument is an argument against that constant.

THE VERSION CLAIM, CONFIRMED FROM THE FULL TEXT. The 14 retrievers are named only by FAMILY -- text: DPR, ColBERT, BGE, E5, Contriever, GTE; visual: DSE-wiki-ss, DSE-docmatix, ColPali, ColQwen; hybrid: four ColPali/ColQwen crossings with ColBERT and BGE -- and NO model version or HuggingFace identifier is given for any of them anywhere. This independently confirms the correction the project made in E34 from inference alone, and it is why every comparison arm here is titled a local paper-style baseline rather than the paper's configuration.

THE NUMBERS THIS PROJECT COMPARES AGAINST. ColQwen image recall is 70.8 / 79.2 / 84.3 at k = 10 / 15 / 20, with ColPali at 68.2 / 77.5 / 81.2. The project measures 0.820 at k=10 on its own candidate pool, above the paper's 0.708, and predicts the gap closes as the image pool is filled out to the paper's scale -- a prediction still unverified because the full-pool index stands at 5 of 220 documents.

MMRAG-DocQA: A Multi-Modal Retrieval-Augmented Generation Method for Document Question-Answering with Hierarchical Index and Multi-Granularity Retrieval

arXiv 2508.00579

THE UNION IS EXPLICIT. Retrieval is defined as the union of the in-page index result and the cross-page index result for every query. Three levels all contribute to the final context: retrieved parent pages, visual content extracted from those pages by an LVLM, and hierarchical summaries built by clustering plus LLM aggregation. Nothing selects among them.

IT IS THEREFORE A CONTROL CASE, NOT A COMPETITOR. Together with VisDoM, which runs visual and textual RAG concurrently and reconciles them, and ViDoRAG, which unions both pipelines, this is the third multimodal system in the registry that pays for everything and merges. That pattern is itself a finding: the multimodal document RAG literature overwhelmingly answers the cost question by not asking it.

IT INDEPENDENTLY SETTLES THE MMLONGBENCH-DOC DISCREPANCY. It cites the benchmark as 135 documents, about 47.5 pages each, 1,082 questions -- the published NeurIPS figures, not the 130 / 1,062 / 49.4 of the arXiv abstract. The proposal currently cites the arXiv numbers and should be corrected.

NUMBERS. MMLongBench-Doc at ten pages: 45.9% accuracy, 46.0 F1. LongDocURL: 57.2% accuracy. Reported as roughly 13 to 15 points over M3DocRAG on MMLongBench-Doc. No computational cost, inference time or latency is reported anywhere.

ViDoRAG: Visual Document Retrieval-Augmented Generation via Dynamic Iterative Reasoning Agents

EMNLP 2025 Main Conference

WHY THE STAGE ASSIGNMENT STAYS AT DEPTH ONLY. The paper states that for each query it retrieves through both the textual and visual pipelines and dynamically determines the optimal top-K with a Gaussian Mixture Model, then unions and re-sorts. So the modality mix is fixed at always-both; only K varies. The conservative assignment made from the abstract was correct, and the modality row of the coverage matrix is unaffected.

THE MECHANISM IS WORTH BORROWING. The GMM fits a bimodal distribution over query-document similarity scores, assumes relevant and irrelevant items come from two Gaussians, and sets K to the number of items assigned to the high-similarity component, with bounds to stop outliers distorting it. That is an unsupervised, label-free way to pick a per-query cutoff -- structurally different from this project's approach of learning a gain predictor from out-of-fold outcomes, and cheaper, because it needs no training signal at all. It is the natural comparator if RQ5 (dynamic top-k) is ever started.

IT REPORTS A COST NUMBER, WHICH IS RARE HERE. Average pages retrieved drops from 10 to 6.76 with the GMM while accuracy is maintained. On ViDoSeek with GPT-4o: ViDoRAG 79.4%, VisualRAG (ColQwen2) 72.1%, TextRAG (NV-Embed-V2) 63.5%, oracle upper bound 87.7%.

DIRECT RELEVANCE TO THIS PROJECT'S COLQWEN RESULT. Its visual-only baseline IS ColQwen2, run at page granularity on slide decks, where it beats text retrieval by 8.6 points. That is the setting in which ColQwen is strong, and it sharpens the boundary this project must state around E24: cropped quote regions inside a question-conditioned candidate pool is not the setting where ColQwen was shown to win.

VisRAG: Vision-based Retrieval-augmented Generation on Multi-modality Documents

ICLR 2025

ITS TEXT BASELINE IS OCR, NOT VLM DESCRIPTIONS -- WHICH LEAVES THIS PROJECT'S COMPARISON UNMADE. TextRAG here uses a MiniCPM retriever over text obtained by PPOCR or model-based parsing. The paper never compares against retrieval over VLM-TRANSCRIBED descriptions of the visual elements. So the specific contrast this project runs -- pixels versus VLM-written image descriptions, same pool, same quotas -- is not answered by VisRAG, and the project can legitimately present it as a comparison the visual-RAG literature has largely skipped. ColPali does make it, at page granularity, and finds strongly for pixels.

THE GAIN IS LARGE AND IS AGAINST PARSING, NOT AGAINST DESCRIPTION. 39% relative improvement over TextRAG with MiniCPM-V 2.6 and 25% with GPT-4o. Read carefully, this is an argument that PARSING loses information, which is the same argument E18 makes in this project when cropped-region OCR scores 0.369 against 0.791 for VLM descriptions. Both results point the same way: extraction is the weak link. Neither settles whether pixels beat a good description.

SINGLE-VECTOR, NOT LATE INTERACTION. Position-weighted mean pooling over the last-layer VLM hidden states gives one embedding per page, so VisRAG is architecturally closer to DSE than to ColPali or ColQwen. For multi-page answering it either concatenates pages into one image or generates per document and selects by combined perplexity and retrieval score.

Adaptive-RAG: Learning to Adapt Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models through Question Complexity

NAACL 2024 (Long Papers), pp. 7036-7050

THE FLAGSHIP ADAPTIVE-RAG PAPER HAS A WEAK ROUTER, AND SAYS SO. Classifier accuracy is 54.52% over three labels, with 31% of multi-step queries misrouted to single-step. The oracle reaches 56.28 F1 while the deployed system reaches 46.94 -- so roughly nine F1 points of the available headroom go unclaimed. This is the closest thing in the literature to the ceiling analysis E35 performs, and it is reported as a number rather than as a controlled experiment.

THE EFFICIENCY CLAIM IS THE SHAPE THIS PROJECT USES. On FLAN-T5-XL, Adaptive-RAG runs at 3.60 s per query against 8.81 s for always-multi-step, averaging 2.17 retrieval steps, at 46.94 F1 against 48.85. On GPT-3.5 it matches multi-step F1 (50.91 vs 50.87) at 1.46 s against 3.33 s. So the headline is the same trade this project reports for ColQwen calls: comparable quality, materially less cost.

WHAT IS MISSING IS WHAT THIS PROJECT ADDS. No random allocation at a matched budget, no tie-aware treatment of the routing outcome, and no statement of what fraction of the oracle advantage the classifier captures beyond the raw F1 gap. The router is also 770M parameters (T5-Large), with ablations down to 60M showing minimal degradation -- worth citing when justifying why this project's routers are small.

Corrective Retrieval Augmented Generation

arXiv 2401.15884; OpenReview JnWJbrnaUE (acceptance not confirmed)

IT IS THE CLOSEST PUBLISHED ANALOGUE TO E36'S CASCADE, AND IT PRICES ITSELF. A T5-large evaluator (0.77B) scores retrieved documents from -1 to 1 and triggers one of three actions: Correct, when at least one document is above the upper threshold, refines the documents by decompose-then-recompose; Incorrect, when all documents fall below the lower threshold, discards them and substitutes web search; Ambiguous combines both. The evaluator reaches 84.3% accuracy against 58-64.7% for ChatGPT. Overhead is reported as roughly +0.15 TFLOPs per token and 0.512 s per instance against 0.363 s for plain RAG -- a 41% time increase for the routing machinery itself, which is exactly the kind of accounting most routing papers omit and which this project's incremental cost model insists on.

NO ORACLE AND NO MATCHED BUDGET. Ablations remove individual actions but there is no upper bound on how well the escalation decision could be made, and no comparison against spending the same escalation budget at random. The gains are real but modest against Self-RAG on the shared LLaMA2-7B setting: PopQA 59.8 against 54.9, Biography FactScore 86.2 against 81.2, PubHealth 74.8 against 72.4, Arc-Challenge 67.2 against 67.3 -- one of four essentially unchanged.

M3DocRAG: Multi-modal Retrieval is What You Need for Multi-page Multi-document Understanding

arXiv preprint (Nov 2024); companion M3DocVQA appears at ICCV 2025 Workshops

IT IS THE CONTROL CASE THE GAP ARGUMENT NEEDS, AND IT REPORTS THE COST NUMBERS MOST PAPERS OMIT. Page retrieval latency across 40,000 pages falls from 21.0 s per query with exact search to 1.8 s with IVFFlat and 0.2 s with IVFPQ. That is the approximate-index lever, and it is orthogonal to routing: it makes the expensive branch cheaper for every query rather than choosing who pays. Any budget claim this project makes should acknowledge that an ANN index is the cheaper first move.

ITS RETRIEVER IS THE ONE THIS PROJECT RUNS. ColPali v1 primary with ColQwen v0.1 as comparison, at PAGE granularity over open-domain corpora -- 3,368 PDFs and 41,005 pages in M3DocVQA. On that benchmark ColPali plus Qwen2-VL reaches 36.5 F1 with four pages against 23.7 for a ColBERTv2 text-RAG pipeline. Together with ViDoRAG and VisRAG this is the third full-text confirmation that late-interaction visual retrieval wins decisively at page granularity in open-domain settings, which is the setting this project does not test and must not claim to have contradicted.

Self-adaptive Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (SAM-RAG)

arXiv 2410.11321; also IEEE conference publication 11227819

THE MECHANISM IS EARLY STOPPING, NOT TOP-K PREDICTION. Retrieved documents are processed in batches; each is judged by an isRel verifier, and retrieval pauses once a batch yields relevant material. Two further verifiers, isUse and isSup, check that the answer addresses the query and is supported by the context. All three are distilled from GPT-4o into LLaVA-7B on instruction data where only reasoning traces whose answers matched the gold were kept, with negatives drawn from DPR-ranked top-50.

ITS COST NUMBER IS THE MOST DIRECTLY COMPARABLE IN THE REGISTRY. Average retrieval number is 1.77 for text against a gold reference of 1.55, and 1.24 for images against 1.00, where conventional RAG fixes k = 8. That is a large cost reduction expressed as closeness to the gold minimum -- a framing this project could borrow, since it is exactly the oracle-relative view E35 builds.

NO CONTROLS AND NO COST ACCOUNTING. There is no random or matched-cost baseline, and no wall-clock or token cost is reported at all, despite the pipeline running three verifier calls per batch on top of retrieval. Comparisons also mix model scales, 7B LLaVA against GPT-4o. So the efficiency claim rests entirely on documents-retrieved, which ignores the verification calls that made the saving possible -- the sunk-cost error this project's incremental accounting is designed to avoid.

Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection

ICLR 2024 (Oral, top 1%)

FOUR REFLECTION TOKENS, TWO OF THEM ROUTING DECISIONS. Retrieve controls whether to fetch passages at all and takes values yes, no or continue; IsRel judges passage relevance; IsSup judges whether the generation is supported; IsUse rates utility on a five-point scale. Decisions are made segment by segment, combining soft weighted scores with hard filtering. So the depth and filter stages are both routed, inside the generation loop rather than before retrieval.

THE EVALUATION GAP IS THE SAME ONE THIS REGISTRY KEEPS FINDING. No oracle, no upper bound, no matched-cost control, and no statistics on retrieval frequency. An ICLR Oral that routes retrieval on demand does not report how much retrieval it saved. Citation precision and recall on ASQA are 70.3 and 71.3.

IT IS ALSO THE BASELINE CRAG BEATS, ON A SHARED RETRIEVER. CRAG holds Contriever fixed and improves on Self-RAG on three of four datasets, which makes the pair the cleanest controlled comparison of two routing designs available in this registry -- and neither of them controls for cost.

Predicting Efficiency/Effectiveness Trade-offs for Dense vs. Sparse Retrieval Strategy Selection

arXiv 2109.10739 (cs.IR)

IT ALREADY USES THIS PROJECT'S CASCADE DESIGN. Two classifiers are trained: sparse versus dense, which sees only the query text, and sparse versus hybrid, which sees the query AND the top-ranked passage returned by an initial sparse retrieval. The second is structurally the cascade of E36 -- run the cheap pass, then decide from its output whether to pay for more. The project cannot present that design as new; what it can present as new is the two-currency budget and the multimodal action space.

AND IT ALREADY USES THIS PROJECT'S PRIMARY CONTROL. Random assignment of queries to strategies at the same budget rate is the paper's main baseline. So matched-budget random allocation is not a novel protocol, and the review's earlier draft claim that it is not routine in retrieval routing is too strong and has been corrected in the gap statement.

THE EFFECT SIZE IS AN ORDER OF MAGNITUDE LARGER THAN THIS PROJECT'S. At a 50% dense budget the classifier reaches 0.95 recall against 0.91 for the random baseline. This project's router beats matched-budget random by 0.006 to 0.011. The gap is not necessarily a failure of the router here: with over 90% of their queries having a single relevant passage, choosing the right retriever flips a nearly all-or-nothing outcome, whereas an MMDocRAG question has several gold quotes and 47.9% of questions have every action tied. That contrast is worth stating explicitly in the report, because it explains why a result that works on passage retrieval need not transfer to multi-gold document retrieval.

NO ORACLE, NO TIE ANALYSIS. Neither an oracle upper bound nor any treatment of tied outcomes appears. The ceiling framing E35 provides has no counterpart here.

Research gap, as the evidence actually supports it

The proposal's gap statement survives, but only once it is scoped and dated. Section 3 claims that current systems "typically adapt only one stage" of the pipeline, and warns that this is preliminary and must be confirmed by review rather than asserted. The review confirms a narrower version: within multimodal document RAG through 2025, the claim holds and is independently corroborated. Stated without that scope, it is false.

The corroboration

An ACL 2026 survey of multimodal RAG for document understanding tabulates 48 systems and identifies no system performing query-conditioned routing across two or more dimensions simultaneously. It characterises the nearest misses explicitly: VisDoMRAG, HM-RAG and ViDoRAG retrieve per modality but combine by union or fusion rather than routing; MG-RAG and MMRAG-DocQA build hierarchical multi-granularity indexes but do not select granularity per query; agent-based systems orchestrate iterative refinement rather than deciding upfront.

This review reached the same conclusion independently before reading the survey. Three multimodal systems were read in full — VisDoM, ViDoRAG and MMRAG-DocQA — and each turned out to pay for everything and merge. ViDoRAG retrieves through both pipelines for every query and unions them, varying only how many items each returns. MMRAG-DocQA defines retrieval as the union of its in-page and cross-page indexes. Union, not selection, is how this field currently handles multiple modalities and granularities — which is precisely the cost structure a budget-framed router would attack.

The counterexample, and why the scope matters

UniversalRAG (ACL 2026) does route modality and granularity together, and must be cited. Its scope is different — text, image and video corpora rather than documents — and it is concurrent with the survey that does not list it. Read in full, it narrows what it refutes in three ways. Its router is a single flat six-way classifier over {None, Paragraph, Document, Image, Clip, Video}, so modality and granularity are one prediction, not a hierarchy. Its labels come from benchmark inductive bias — queries from a dataset meant to be answered parametrically are labelled "None" — which is a proxy, not a measured outcome. And it reports no cost at all: no latency, no compute, no pass counts.

Where the ground is genuinely taken

Retrieval depth is crowded — eight of 36 works, including Adaptive-RAG, Self-RAG, CRAG and SAM-RAG. The cost-framed retriever question was asked in 2021: Arabzadeh, Yan and Clarke train a per-query classifier over sparse, dense and hybrid, motivated by cutting GPU calls, using a cascade design — the sparse-versus-hybrid classifier sees the query and the top passage from an initial sparse pass — and using matched-budget random allocation as its primary baseline. Both the cascade and the control this project treats as central are precedented there. Granularity routing exists for text in Mix-of-Granularity and RAPTOR.

The concurrent result

Modality Relevance is not Modality Utility (July 2026) independently reaches several of this project's conclusions on MultiModalQA: the same cost asymmetry, the same finding that modality relevance poorly predicts modality need, and the same observation that a pre-retrieval router escalating on apparent visual relevance over-escalates against an oracle. Its utility oracle reaches 0.469 accuracy at a 12% escalation rate, above the 0.446 of an always-on vision pipeline. That 12% sits in the same range as this project's 0.05–0.15, on a different corpus and metric; the agreement in order of magnitude is corroboration and should be reported as such rather than buried.

What remains open, after 15 close readings

  1. Nobody prices two retrieval currencies that have no exchange rate. This statement was narrowed on 2026-09-01 after MAGE-RAG was read. The original claim — that cost, where reported at all, collapses to one scalar — is no longer defensible: MAGE-RAG carries a seven-element budget vector and reports 20.99 images, 4.05 context pages and 3.23 LLM calls per sample, with efficiency as 0.162 score per LLM call. What survives is narrower and still unmet. Its budget dimensions are quantities of evidence inside one pipeline, all convertible into reader context. No work read here separates a CPU retrieval pass from a GPU late-interaction pass and then declines to sum them because no exchange rate between the two was measured. ColPali supplies the reason that separation matters: a page costs 256 KB of index against 8.6 KB for BGE-M3, while query encoding differs by only 30 ms against 22 ms — the two costs do not scale together, so one number cannot represent both.
  2. Hierarchy, as opposed to a flat joint label or a free-running agent. A second counterexample was added on 2026-09-01. UniversalRAG's six-way classifier is flat: modality and granularity are one prediction. R1-Router (arXiv 2505.22095, retitled Mixture-of-Retrieval Experts in its 2026 revision) is the other end — it routes each intermediate query to whichever knowledge base suits the evolving reasoning state, trained by Step-GRPO, which is adaptive and multi-step but has no fixed decision structure at all and operates over open-domain knowledge bases rather than the retrievers available for one document. So the field has a flat joint label at one extreme and an unconstrained agent at the other; a declared hierarchy of a few cheap decisions, each priced, is still absent. Neither work reports a retrieval-call count or a latency figure: R1-Router's v1 abstract claims it is “reducing unnecessary retrievals and improving both efficiency and accuracy” without quantifying it, and the v2 abstract drops the sentence.
  3. Proxy labels are the norm. UniversalRAG labels by dataset identity; Adaptive-RAG falls back to the same shortcut where outcome labels are unavailable, and its classifier reaches only 54.52% accuracy, leaving roughly nine F1 points of its own oracle unclaimed. Training on measured per-question outcomes, which this project adopted only after correcting E17, is not standard practice.
  4. Oracles and tie-awareness are almost absent. Of the 15 papers read in full, only Adaptive-RAG reports an oracle at all, and none reports a tie-aware oracle, a permutation control over action marginals, or the fraction of oracle advantage a router captures. MAGE-RAG, the most cost-conscious system in this registry, reports none of the three either. Recall at k is a ratio of small integers, so ties dominate: 47.9% of this project's questions have every action tied.
  5. Routing machinery is rarely charged for. CRAG is the honest exception, reporting +0.15 TFLOPs per token and 0.512 s against 0.363 s — a 41% overhead for the routing decision itself. Self-RAG reports no cost and never says how often it skips retrieval. SAM-RAG reports documents retrieved (1.77 against a gold 1.55) while omitting the three verifier calls that produced the saving.
  6. Fusion is the missing baseline. UniversalRAG's strongest comparator is a shared-embedding corpus its own analysis says suffers a modality gap. Nothing compares a router against retrieving every modality and fusing the ranked lists — the static baseline this project must beat, and does not.

A boundary this review imposes on the project's own claims

ColPali's baselines include a captioning pipeline in which visual elements are described by Claude-3 Sonnet and retrieved as text. ColPali reaches 81.3 NDCG@5 against roughly 67 for captioning — about fourteen points in favour of pixels over VLM-written descriptions, at page granularity on whole pages. This project measures no such advantage for ColQwen over image-description retrieval, but it measures it on cropped quote regions inside a question-conditioned candidate pool with a median of about twenty image candidates per document, where recall at 20 is near saturation. The project's result must be stated as bounded to that regime. It must not be generalised into a claim that descriptions match pixels.

VisRAG, notably, never makes this comparison at all: its text baseline is OCR and parser output, not VLM transcription. So the specific contrast this project runs is one the visual-RAG literature has largely skipped — which is a contribution, provided it is scoped.

Scope of this claim. 36 works, 8 themes, 36 recorded queries across the ACL Anthology, NeurIPS, ICLR/OpenReview, ACM DL, IEEE Xplore, AAAI and arXiv. Fifteen are read in full and are set out above; the other 21 are recorded at abstract level and labelled as such, so anything resting on them is provisional in the way an abstract is. Two corrections were forced during the review and are recorded in the affected entries: a claim that the Modality-Utility paper reports no random control was withdrawn after reading its Table 2, and an apparent finding that no prior work routes modality turned out to be an artefact of not having searched for it. Vendor blogs and LLM-generated summary aggregators were used only as pointers to primary sources and are never cited.

The four outstanding leads were resolved on 2026-09-01, and two of them were one paper. “R1-Router” and “Mixture-of-Retrieval Experts” are the same arXiv submission (2505.22095) under two titles, renamed by the same authors between v1 (2025-05-28) and v2 (2026-04-06) around the same Step-GRPO method. Recording them separately would have counted one system twice in the coverage matrix. A lead list assembled from titles can contain duplicates that only a version check reveals, which is worth stating because nothing in the search results themselves flagged it.

The three resulting records are abstract-level and labelled as such. Reading them changed two of the six statements above: MAGE-RAG forced statement (1) to be narrowed, because it does price more than one dimension; R1-Router was added to statement (2) as a second counterexample. CogPlanner changed nothing — it plans query reformulation rather than selecting a retriever or a modality, and like most of this registry it claims efficiency without quantifying it. Statement (4) was reinforced rather than weakened: MAGE-RAG reports no oracle, no matched-budget random control and no tie analysis.

Reciprocal Rank Fusion Outperforms Condorcet and Individual Rank Learning Methods
SIGIR 2009 (32nd ACM SIGIR), Boston MA · read: abstract
—textrank fusion over several retrieval systemsTREC experiments, LETOR 3not readTHE SOURCE OF THE FUSION RULE THIS PROJECT USES. A document score is the sum of reciprocals of its ranks across systems, scaled by a constant k; the paper shows this beats every individual system and beats Condorcet Fuse, with no training and no score normalisation. That is precisely why RRF is the static baseline the project's router has to beat rather than a strawman -- it is a seventeen-year-old parameter-free method that is very hard to improve on, and E36 confirms a learned router does not.
